# Watchly — Product Design

The product compass. Every feature, redesign, and prioritization call is
checked against this page. Agents and panels (product analyst, product
engineer, UI/UX) start here.

## Core philosophy

Watchly answers two questions, and everything else serves them:

1. **Where can I watch this movie or show?**
2. **What should I watch tonight?**

## North star

> Watchly should be useful enough that people don't need its social
> features, but personal enough that they'll eventually want to share it
> with others.

Utility comes first. Social is something people grow into, never a
requirement for getting value.

## Personality

Personality should enhance the utility, never obstruct it. Charm (copy,
motion, cinematic touches) is welcome only when the task is still as fast
and clear as it would be without it.

## The three checks

Every feature or change has to pass:

1. Does this make it easier to **find or decide** what to watch?
2. Does this improve the experience of **organizing and enjoying**
   entertainment?
3. Does this reinforce Watchly's **identity** without adding unnecessary
   complexity?

If the answer to all three is no, don't build it. If it only passes the
third, keep it small.

## How we measure (product analyst / product engineer)

| Question | What we measure |
| --- | --- |
| Can people find what they want? | Search success and time to find a title |
| Does Watchly help them decide? | Title-to-provider click-through |
| Are discovery features useful? | Swipe and For You engagement leading to saves or watches |
| Do people care about collections? | Watchlist creation and repeat visits |
| Is the experience reliable? | Error rates, real-user load times, and regressions |
| Do people come back? | 7-day and 30-day retention |

Success metrics for any new feature should map to one of these rows. Raw
engagement (time spent, taps) is never the goal on its own.
